The complete front/profile/back views depict a lightly fitted olive-gray long tunic with continuous set-in sleeves, restrained shoulder shape, loose elbow fabric and leather cuffs. A soft cowl crosses the front neckline between two round shoulder brooches; shallow folds hang at the rear without covering the head. The cape wraps onto the shoulder sides and down toward the front edges; its four principal vertical folds affect the back/profile silhouette. A belt with one working buckle and a second hanger ring cinches the same long garment; the hanging tongue lies against its front. The lower garment has two front edges and a real open slit, with trousers and boots visible through/below it.

The old two diagonal leather chest bands, three metal buttons and straight upper-chest bar are not matching details in the approved complete reference. Do not leave those pieces in place and add a new cowl on top. Replace the relevant old fitting pieces with the actual center placket/cowl attachment/belt structure. Old circular torso and sleeve rings with arbitrary sinusoidal ripples are a starting cage only, not a finished reproduction of the new shoulders, armholes and elbow folds. Old cape geometry is a rear fan-like sheet and the keeper skirt a closed three-ring tube; both require a different actual surface layout.

Register the complete image to the existing root/floor/head-height landmarks, then register garment parts using the same shoulder/elbow/wrist/waist/knee/ankle anchors; do not independently stretch each part to make its own panel look correct. Only perimeter/intersection-visible thickness needs explicit edge strips initially; doubling every garment triangle as a full inner shell is not the default. Thin seam/thread details should not become hundreds of arbitrary tubes. Major folds, open edges and fitted proportions must first be present in mesh geometry; the sheet is not pasted on as a substitute.

Proposed new data format: per-part positions/indices, normal and physical-UV corner attributes, semantic seam IDs (`neckCut`, `armhole0/1`, `cuff0/1`, `waist`, `capeTop`, `frontOpening0/1`), and up to four normalized influences by existing bone name per vertex. Store image hashes and common 2D landmarks/3D rig anchors in the authoring provenance. Use deterministic authored panel/control curves sampled to a mesh, not random fold noise. Sewn boundary positions and their weights are copied from a single shared boundary record so later edits cannot separate the parts.

Current `bindArticulatedSkin` derives weights from a one-axis rule and overwrites attributes. An arbitrary welded armhole/gusset therefore needs a minimal explicit-per-vertex influence path, guarded by Mira-specific metadata, or an equivalent focused builder that creates the final material skin batches. Preserve the existing path byte/geometry behavior for the other13families and preserve per-instance bones/shared immutable geometry. This is a likely source-edit requirement to authorize explicitly; it is not already implemented.

Current `upperCloth()` assumes torso18segments, sleeve12segments and ring-major vertex order. It cannot be run unchanged on a new arbitrary topology. Author physical-distance charts on the new panels, keep the256mm material scale and same material slots, and preserve UV seam attributes rather than welding away texture corners. The existing procedural map's good physical yarn scale does not recreate the references' full basecolor/weathering/material response; that remaining material comparison must stay visible in the completion record.

Current cape animator infers column count by top vertices with local y>.479, assumes row-major meridians and rebuilds death contact from its indexed edges. It also reconstructs free meridians toward a common forward direction. New side-wrap folds can be valid in idle yet fail during death or arm motion. The first candidate should keep that grid contract, compare idle/walk/run and existing explicit death phases using the real new surface, and report any need for a **separately authorized** Mira-only contact adaptation. No silent global cape/animation changes are planned. The old top9 count is not itself hard-coded; the y threshold/order assumptions are real contracts.

## Implementation order toward the complete person

1. Freeze assembled waist/neck/cape interpretation, common rig registration and the whole six-sheet completion checklist. Allocate separate head/hair, clothing and hands/boots/staff ownership; all part writers share the same head/neck, wrist/cuff and ankle/boot boundaries.
2. Build upper tunic, sleeves and the front joining details as one real surface assembly. Match front, both sides and obliques after the real staff-holding pose. Remove incompatible old front pieces. This is the first integration unit, explicitly **not** the completed person.
3. Assemble cowl/shoulders, full mantle, split lower panels and complete belt into that same upper assembly. Verify full front/rear/sides at a common scale; a good detached part view cannot substitute for this whole-character comparison.
4. Integrate the independently built head/hair and any hand/boot/staff replacements. Keep an old part only after a corresponding actual multi-angle comparison passes; no blanket statement that the unchanged old face or primitive staff already matches. Face/cowl and hand/cuff intersections are assembly acceptance, not merely per-part checks.
5. Compare the full assembled native actor in the actual three dialogue viewports and fixed complete-character views. Inspect the authorized real rendering when available through the parent. Require visible contour/material/part correspondence and motion continuity, then store the remaining mismatches. Triangles or sheets completed are not the completion criterion.
